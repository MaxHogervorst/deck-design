Deck and step build guide

10 November 2026 · How I am going to build the 6.6 m deck and the low step in front of it. One plan, three drawings, a priced shopping list and the build order, written for an owner doing the work. It is not an engineer's design; the things I have assumed, and would ideally have checked, are listed at the end.

Foundations

16 timber piles: 12 ordinary (holes 350×350, 450 deep) and 4 corner anchors (holes 350×350, 900 deep) with 12 kN fixing kits. One pour of about 0.99 m³ fills every hole to 100 mm below ground; nothing in it needs to be level, because the piles are cut to a string afterwards. A mini-mix truck for 1.2 m³ is the easy way; 104 bags of 20 kg and a hired mixer is the big-box way.

The step does not get concrete. Each of the six pads is a pit 450×800 mm dug to firm ground, filled with compacted GAP7, with two 300×300 concrete pavers bedded on top, one under each rail. The rails sit on the pavers on DPC and plastic packers. The pads are between the pile holes so the pavers sit on undisturbed ground.

Why the step sits on pavers

A timber pile has to stand at least 150 mm clear of the ground, and under a 250 mm step there is only about 78 mm, so short piles are out. Pouring six little concrete pads to exactly the same level is the fiddliest job on the site for someone who has not done it before. Pavers on a tamped base, with the deck frame as the level reference and packers to take up the difference, is how most low steps beside a deck are actually built. The step is 89% of my bending screen with a 2.7 kN person standing on the very end, which is the hardest case.

Setting the pavers without a level

  1. Build the deck frame first. Its rim is straight and level, so every step measurement is a tape held down from the rim top.
  2. Dig each pit to firm ground, fill with GAP7 in two layers and tamp each one hard. Bed the two pavers and tap them down until the top is 400 to 420 mm below the rim top (about +50 to +70 above the lawn). Lower is fine, higher is not.
  3. Build the step frame on the lawn, lift it onto the pavers against the cleats, and pack under each rail at each paver until the frame top is 250 mm below the rim top everywhere and nothing rocks. Then screw the cleats on.

Build order

  1. Before you start. Check where the deck top will sit against the house: it must stay below the door sill and clear of the bottom of the cladding by whatever the cladding maker asks for, so measure the real door and cladding, not a drawing. Call beforeUdig (0800 248 344) or check the property file for pipes and cables where the holes go. Agree the two datums with yourself: deck boards top at +500 and step boards top at +250 above the lawn.
  2. Set out. String the 6600×2600 outline, the 900×1970 rebuilt strip and the 400 mm step. Mark the 16 pile holes from Drawing 1. Do not mark the paver pits yet; they are dug after the deck frame is up. Leave the lawn and path at their present levels.
  3. Dig. Twelve ordinary pile holes 350×350 and 450 deep, four corner anchor holes 350×350 and 900 deep. Dig to firm natural ground, not topsoil; if a hole is still soft or wet at depth, stop and read the assumptions at the end.
  4. Pour. Stand all 16 piles long and plumb, brace them with offcuts, and fill every hole to about 100 mm below ground in one go, about 0.99 m³. Order 1.2 m³ from a mini-mix truck, or mix 104 bags of 20 kg (buy 115). Nothing in this pour needs to be level; the piles get cut later.
  5. Cut the piles. After the concrete has set, string a line at +187 mm (lawn = 0) along each pile row with a water level or laser, mark every pile and cut square. Brush preservative on the cuts. Lay a square of DPC on each pile top.
  6. Bearers. Nail up the two 6.6 m main double bearers and two 0.9 m rebuilt double bearers (F1). Seat them on the piles at Y300 and Y2510, and at Y−1925 and Y−800, and skew-nail (F2). Fit the four corner anchor kits to the sheet in the box. Take out the old rear bearer; do not reuse it.
  7. Joists and rims. 17 main joists of 2510 mm at the centres in the set-out list, with the pair at 3933/3978. Three rebuilt joists of 1880 mm, two blocks over the Y−800 bearer and the three SSCPC40 hold-down pairs at the rear (F4). Nail the 6.6 m rims at Y0 and Y2555 and the 900 mm rebuilt rims. Two M12 rim ties where the rebuilt strip meets the main deck (F6).
  8. Step cleats. Screw three 250 mm H4 cleats to the outside face of the front bearer at X1000, 3300, 5600, bottoms at +78 (that is 390 mm down from the rim top), with two 14g×100 screws each (F5).
  9. Paver pits. Now that the deck frame is up and level, dig six pits 450×800 mm centred at X420, 1765, 3060, 3540, 4835, 6180, Y2800, about 150 mm deep or until you reach firm ground. Fill with GAP7 in two layers, tamping each hard with the end of a pile offcut. Bed two 300×300 pavers in each pit, one under each rail line (Y2645 and Y2955), and tap them down with a rubber mallet until the top is 400 to 420 mm below the deck rim top, measured with a straight edge and tape. Flat matters; exactly level does not.
  10. Step frame. Nail up the two 6.6 m double H4 rails (F1 pattern). Lay the inner-face ply of each rail on the lawn, nail the 17 crosspieces of 220 mm between them at the joist centres (F3), then nail the outer plies on. Lift the frame onto the pavers with the inner rail against the cleats. At each paver, slide DPC and plastic packers under the rail until the frame top is exactly 250 mm below the deck rim top and the frame does not rock. Screw each cleat to the inner rail (F5).
  11. Boards. Lay the 32 mm boards along X on the main deck, rebuilt strip and step. Put every butt joint on the paired members at 3955. Use the board supplier's gap and two screws per board per joist. Brush every cut. Finish with lawn, path and garden clear of all timber.

Assumptions, and what I would get checked in an ideal world

This is what I would do. None of it has been checked by an engineer or a builder, and no council consent is involved: the deck is under 1.5 m, so it is exempt building work, but it still has to be built properly.

  • The four corner piles as anchors. A freestanding deck needs something to stop it racking. My default is four corner anchor piles with 12 kN kits in 900 mm holes. An engineer would size this properly from the deck weight and the site wind and earthquake loads; I am relying on it being a common detail for a low deck this size.
  • The ground. Every footing assumes firm natural clay at about 100 kPa under the topsoil, which is normal for Auckland but not guaranteed. The pads put at most 37 kPa on the pavers. If a hole hits soft, wet or filled ground, that hole needs a deeper or wider footing, and someone who knows soils should look at it.
  • The step rails. The double H4 rails are checked as a beam only, at 89% of my screen with a 2.7 kN point load on the very end, using the grade stresses for wet timber. The pavers, packers and cleats are ordinary carpentry, not rated connections. When someone stands on the very end of the step the rail may lift a millimetre or two off the next paver along; that is expected and harmless, but it is a reason not to leave big gaps under the rails.
  • No hold-down on the step. The step is held to the deck by three cleats and otherwise sits by gravity. I think that is fine for a 250 mm high step with boards on it; a builder might add a bracket at each paver anyway.
  • Stainless everywhere. I have priced 316 stainless nails, screws and kits because I do not know if the site is in the sea-spray zone (within 500 m of the sea or a harbour, or 100 m of a tidal estuary or sheltered inlet). Outside it, NZS 3604 allows hot-dip galvanised, which is about a quarter of the price. Worth checking on the Auckland Council GIS viewer before buying fasteners.
  • The house junction. I have not drawn the gap to the cladding or the drop from the door sill. Both depend on the actual cladding and door, so step 1 of the build order is to measure them. Keep the deck below the door sill and keep timber off the cladding so water can get out.
  • The 250 mm step. A single 250 mm rise is above what the stair rules allow for a compliant step. It is our choice for this garden, and nothing here claims access compliance.
  • Timber and prices. 140×45 H4 is not listed online at either store; if the store only has rough-sawn 150×50 H4, use the note in the shopping list. Prices were read on 10 November 2026 and will drift; the two estimates (7.2 m framing and the pavers) are what the store will tell me in person.
  • The old rebuilt strip. I am replacing the old bearer and not relying on any retained timber. If what comes out of the ground is worse than expected, the rebuilt piles get the same treatment as the main ones.
