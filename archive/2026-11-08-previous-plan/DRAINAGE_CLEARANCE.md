## What remains unknown on flat grass

Normal bearing ground can still hold water. Removing100mm from a flat area may leave a shallow basin surrounded by higher lawn. Grass, gravel or weed mat alone does not give that basin an outlet. Previous successful house piles support the bearing-ground assumption but do not locate a drainage outlet.

The full-area100mm draft would remove about**1.89m³** of soil over the main plus rebuilt footprints, before foundation holes, actual turf/topsoil variation, slope and disposal bulking. This is an indication of the proposal's size, not an excavation order. Local pockets are not an automatic shortcut: they also need drainage.

## Simple on-site resolution — no photographs needed

1. Mark the proposed−100mm under-deck level with a laser, water level or level string, using the adjacent garden/path as0. Check the actual low points, existing water flow and the six anchor locations.
2. Identify where water could leave that formation. Measure whether a lawful outlet exists sufficiently lower to provide positive fall, allowing for the actual drain/outlet detail. Keep runoff away from the house. A drawing cannot establish that outlet from “flat grass”.
3. If a workable outlet exists, have the builder/designer show the formation fall, outlet and any drain on the agreed plan. Measure the lowest soil clearance at the actual completed anchor cuts; hole depths and pile cut lengths follow that formation.
4. **If no outlet exists, do not simply dig a100mm basin.** Revise the low main anchor/ground detail before excavation. A soakage system would require site-specific support; do not assume a gravel trench, weed mat or general garden soil will dispose of the water.

The planning proposal therefore stays **conditional:100mm lowering only with a workable drainage detail**. The owner has no further height/look preference to choose. The unresolved fact is a physical level/outlet check on site, which can be included in the one pre-build discussion. No construction-phase builder inspections are added.
