## Decision and load path

CONFIRMED BY USER: simplify the tread if clearances are poor. Retain 6600 × 400 mm, surface +250 mm, 32 mm pine and structural H5 timber posts concreted in excavated holes within the tread footprint. No new foundation system is proposed.

PROVISIONAL recommendation: run approximately 400 mm decking pieces **front to back**, on two wide, shallow longitudinal timber bearers. Each bearer is **two 90 × 70 SG8 H5 members on edge**, making 90 mm deep × 140 mm wide. Both are directly seated on 125 × 125 H5 structural piles. This removes the ladder crosspieces, their hangers, and the vertical-load-carrying rail/post bolts from the previous proposal. The wider bearers keep the board end projections small while permitting the pile rows to move inboard.

Vertical load path: board → timber bearer → DPC → pile top → concrete footing → verified bearing soil. Fixings retain the seated frame. They do not suspend it beside the piles.

CONFIRMED BY USER this pass: **connect the tread to the freestanding deck frame for sideways restraint**, with the tread's own H5 posts carrying its weight. No support or attachment to the house is introduced. The exact connection and combined deck bracing need checking. Do not call the small ordinary gravity footings anchor footings.

## Fixings and supported joints

VERIFIED WITH PRIMARY SOURCE: NZS 3604:2011 §6.5.2(b) includes an ordinary timber-pile connection using **four 100 × 3.75 mm skew nails** as an alternative to the wire-dog arrangement. Develop the stainless version appropriate to the actual exposure and supplied fasteners. Keep the nail entries/cut surfaces and the actual supplied pile manufacturer's processing conditions satisfied. This is an ordinary connection, not a 12 kN anchor connection. Joint piles, split risk, nail clashes and any required uplift need their specific fixing check; do not order an exact nail count from four times fourteen alone.

For two 70 mm bearer plies, §2.4.4.7 requires at least 52.5 mm penetration into the second ply and nails at no more than 420 mm along the member, alternating faces. A 125 mm nail gives 55 mm nominal penetration; a 100 mm nail is too short. Develop one row at mid-depth, **125 mm stainless structural nails at 300 mm maximum**, with actual nail diameter, availability, end/edge distances and seam patterns confirmed. Do not substitute screws without a supported structural fastening schedule. This is a fastening basis, not a verified supplier SKU.

Stagger bearer-ply joints over existing piles, so there is no extra pile solely for a rail joint:

- Rear plies: seams at X2175 / X4225 respectively.
- Front plies: seams at X2400 / X4450 respectively.

Each individual ply has multiple supported spans on both sides of its joint. Nominal butt-end bearing is 62.5 mm each before the agreed end gap, exceeding the 45 mm minimum for a supported butt joint. The unjointed ply continues through each joint location. Check seam fixation and actual cuts before fabrication.

For board fixings, retain the working basis **75 mm 316 stainless decking screws, two per board per bearer**, predrilled as appropriate. Exact head/gauge, fixing lines and end/edge rules follow the actual board and screw instructions. These screws are not pile or structural framing fasteners.

## Lateral restraint — one connection to finish, not an invented rating

Ordinary piles do not supply a rated bracing system. Develop four simple vertical timber drop-cleats between the main deck's front rim and the rear tread bearer, with through-bolted interfaces. A normal steel hanger alone cannot bridge the nominal 110 mm vertical gap between the main rim bottom (+328) and tread framing top (+218).

Dimensional review envelope: H5 190 × 45 drop-cleats, about 390 mm long, top +468 / bottom about +78; 45 mm thickness plus a 5 mm durable packer fits the 50 mm horizontal gap between deck face and rear tread bearer. Two M10 through-bolts per interface, 75 mm horizontal separation, candidate levels +380 (deck) / +173 (tread), washers both timber faces. Candidate bolt lengths are 150 mm at the deck and 250 mm at the tread, subject to actual packs, washers, nuts and threads. Four cleats would have 16 bolt positions. Choose locations clear of main joists, pile connections and bearer seams; add the necessary main-frame load transfer/blocking in the checked detail.

These dimensions show a plain timber connection can physically bridge the gap. **They are not a verified bolt-group, cleat-bending, rim or bracing capacity.** A qualified NZ structural designer must check the detail and the combined deck bracing before construction. Do not publish the candidate bolt sizes/count as an approved fixing schedule. The owner has confirmed lateral connection to the deck; its hardware and structural capacity remain provisional.
