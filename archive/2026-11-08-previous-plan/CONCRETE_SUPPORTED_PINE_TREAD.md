## Approach selected for development

Keep the main/rebuilt deck's conventional timber framing and timber-pile development. For the low tread, use a **single-layer timber box directly bearing on low concrete pads/plinths**, with impervious separation and ordinary proprietary stainless restraint connections fixed to the concrete. The vertical load is carried by bearing, not by an offset cleat attached to the main deck.

There are **no cut-off timber piles underneath this tread**, so the150 mm timber-pile cut/top requirement does not govern its support height. Timber durability, drainage, anchorage and foundation capacity still apply. This is the low-bearer/concrete-pad method discussed in BRANZ Bulletin611§§5.1.3–5.1.4; that reference recognises the method, not the final pad or connection dimensions of this project.

The owner allowed lateral attachment to the deck previously. The objective here is a stable, positively anchored tread on its own concrete supports, avoiding reliance on the unverified drop-cleat proposal. **Do not declare independent lateral/uplift resistance verified until the pad/anchor assembly is checked.** No custom steel heads or offset timber drop-cleats are selected.

**X300 /1300 /2300 /3300 /4300 /5300 /6300 mm**.

This gives1000 mm maximum centre intervals and300 mm end projections to the support reference centres. The actual bearing faces reduce the clear rail spans. These positions are freshly derived for this box; they are not the old seven-pole/custom-head layout or the withdrawn14-pile schedule.

The support body may extend below the adjacent landscaping; the top bearing region must support both rails, accommodate the chosen anchors' edge distances and fit the visible finish. Draw its final below-ground width/depth, raised profile, concrete grade, any reinforcement and anchor placement from the actual soil and product detail. **No pad dimensions, volume or reinforcement quantity are released here.** A generic rectangle whose geometry fits is not a verified footing.

The preferred finish is the simple exposed front rail and discreet concrete supports. Do not promise all concrete is invisible from every angle or cover a drainage crown with soil at the timber interface. If a plain fascia is needed to screen hardware, add one simple treated board with a drainage/ventilation opening; no decorative enclosed skirt is assumed.

## Preliminary member-demand screen

Use the corrected domestic-stair concentrated action2.7 kN. A conservative whole factored point4.05 kN at midspan of a1000 mm rail bay givesM*=1.0125 kNm, before permanent action. For a140×90 built-up rail,Z=294,000 mm³ and gross stress3.44 MPa. A220 mm clear crosspiece under the same entire point gives0.22275 kNm and1.52 MPa gross stress. Broad bearing/continuity can reduce those demands, but no credit is required to obtain these screening figures.

These are demand calculations, not a full resistance check. Include actual edge load distribution, the applicable independent-tread line action, wet timber factors, supported joints, anchorage and soil in the final check. No brand strength is assigned to the unknown decking. Normal wet-service table/member factors and product ratings still govern.

## Fixing basis

- Impervious separation between concrete and timber; preserve drainage around the support.
- Stainless framing/restraint hardware suitable for the exposure andH4 treatment, minimum304 where the BRANZ low-bearer detail applies.
- Direct timber→proprietary bracket→concrete restraint. Specify the complete matched bracket, timber fasteners and concrete anchors together, with actual capacity in the relevant directions. Concrete thickness, edge distances and spacing are part of that selection.
-75 mm316 stainless decking screws remain the working length basis for32 mm pine, with actual head/gauge/pilot/edge rules confirmed for the boards.

The initial product research found generic angles and bracket families, but **did not establish one fully applicable complete rated pad-to-box connection**. No specific M10/M12 count or angle capacity is invented in this recommendation. This is the remaining bounded fixing specification, to be resolved against the selected support dimensions and manufacturer's data before construction.

## Finish and owner sequence

Use a natural treated-pine appearance. Required cut-end treatment remains part of installation. Start with the exposed front framing face, sound board edges and neatly formed concrete; add only the plain fascia required for appearance. No stain/paint, decorative trim or elaborate skirt is included in the base finish plan; actual supplied timber instructions still apply.

After the pre-build support/anchor/site details are settled, use one sequence: set all datums → excavate main foundations and tread pad locations → prepare and pour concrete with the support levels/formwork set correctly → cure to the applicable loading/anchor requirements → assemble the box and direct pad restraint → fit boards → finish the adjacent path/grass/garden. No builder inspections during construction are introduced.

## Remaining facts, without another owner-preference questionnaire

The owner has settled the important choices: keep pine, keep height, choose simpler supports and minimal finishing. Actual bearing ground, levels/drainage, path interface and the complete pad/anchor assembly require the pre-build site/product check. Timber/board supply and quotes then determine the order. No old budget, post count or concrete volume is reused, and no unquoted claim of the lowest total price is made.
