## 1. Main doubled-bearer ply fastening

**Verified rule:** NZS3604:2011 §2.4.4.7 permits matching built-up members, subject to ply dimensions/grade, nailing and last-ply penetration. For two45 mm plies, the maximum nail spacing is270 mm, minimum penetration into the second ply33.75 mm, and140 mm depth needs two nail rows.

**Developed fastening basis:**90 ×4 mm stainless structural nails, two rows at250 mm maximum along each row, driven alternately from opposite faces. Nominal row heights35 /105 mm from the bearer bottom and50 mm end offsets are a dimensional layout for the final fixing drawing.90 mm length gives45 mm nominal second-ply penetration. Verify actual supplied dimensions, nail product, end/edge rules, splitting/predrilling and clashes at all plates and splices.

This resolves the general ply-stitching geometry; it does not provide a rated anchor connection or splice. Keep the splice/connector zones free until the complete joint drawing fixes their nails.

## 2. Ordinary timber pile to seated bearer

**Verified prescriptive routes:** §6.5.2(b) includes four100 ×3.75 mm skew nails, or the prescribed nail/wire-dog connection. MiTek OPF's complete manufacturer detail uses two90 ×4 mm stainless skew nails plus two stainless wire dogs, one per side, bent as shown for square piles.

For the simple ordinary-pile design, develop the four100 ×3.75 stainless skew-nail route where nail product, skew direction, timber end distances and surface processing height are satisfied. The ten deck/rebuilt ordinary piles must remain distinct from the six proposed anchor piles. A splice post still requires the splice connection as well as ordinary pile attachment. No exact order count is released until these joints are drawn.

The 150 mm processing condition concerns the actual processed pile surfaces/entries, not simply the bolt or nail-head centre. Check the exact supplied pile instructions. At the nominal main pile-top+186, top cuts can clear150 on ground at0, but a wire-dog or anchor detail extending down the pile may not. Do not assume this top-height calculation verifies the whole connector.

## 3. Ordinary joist to bearer, and rim to joist

**Verified NZS3604 Table7.5 hand-driven schedule:**

| Ordinary joint | Standard hand-driven nail detail |
|---|---|
| Joist seated on bearer |2 ×100 ×3.75 mm skew nails at the bearing |
| Boundary/rim to each joist end |2 ×100 ×3.75 mm end nails |
| Solid blocking to a joist |2 ×100 ×3.75 mm end nails, or4 ×75 ×3.15 mm skew nails |
| Blocking down to a bearer/plate where that fixing applies |4 ×100 ×3.75 mm skew nails |

Specify the appropriate stainless products for this exposed low deck. Do not replace these nail schedules with decking screws, and do not treat a larger or differently shaped nail as an automatically identical specified product. The power-driven column is a different schedule: for example three90 ×3.15 nails for an ordinary joist/bearer bearing, rather than two hand-driven nails.

There are50 proposed joist/bearer intersections from the present geometry:21 main joists including sisters ×2 main bearers, plus4 continuous joists ×2 extra rebuilt bearers. **Some are part of rated anchor connections and need that complete detail instead.** This is not100 ordinary nails to order indiscriminately.

Full-depth structural lateral blocks can follow the solid-blocking schedule when used for that purpose. Short rebuilt members carrying rotated decking loads must be classified correctly: Table7.5 separately lists three100 ×3.75 end nails for a curtailed joist up to3 m to a trimmer. A generic two-nail blocking rule is not automatically the right release for every load-carrying rebuilt cross-member. The full short-member/trimmer arrangement, access and opposed-nail clashes need its joint drawing; stainless hangers remain a possible rated detail only if their actual fit/wet capacity is established. Do not order dozens of hangers from a diagram alone.

## 4. Lateral support near the anchor lines

**Verified rule:** §7.1.2 requires the relevant lines of joist lateral support within300 mm of subfloor horizontal support/bracing lines. Full-depth blocks/strutting and boundary-joist provisions are defined there.

The proposed main full-depth block rows atY600 /2000 are each250 mm from bearer linesY350 /2250. Their **positions satisfy that300 mm proximity comparison**. They still need the correct end fixings and a complete load path, particularly where tread ties introduce load at the front rim. Good positioning does not make unspecified joints rated.

## 5. Bearer splices — a real requirement, not just ply nails

**Verified rule:** §6.12.7.1 allows bearer joints over supports and prohibits them directly at anchor/braced piles. §6.12.7.2 requires a rated axial splice:12 kN for a complete joint, or6 kN on one side when one laminate continues over the support.

The current main bearer seams atX3910 are over ordinary piles, so they avoid the prohibited anchor positions. Because both main plies are proposed to end there, do not treat the detail as the single-continuous-laminate6 kN exception.

MiTek publishes an actual **12 kN bearer-splice-over-pile** detail:160 mm Nailon plate, eight45 mm specified nails into each end, with18 mm minimum timber-edge /45 mm timber-end exclusion and the ordinary pile fixing separately. Use the stainless plate/nails for this environment. The published plate's rated assembly and the transfer into **both45 mm plies** must be shown in the final joint. The source is not permission to halve the nails, assume an arbitrary plate is12 kN or declare a one-face plate to connect both plies automatically.

The new staggered tread-ply seams also remain subject to their applicable rated splice requirement; staggering avoids cutting both plies at one support but does not eliminate all splice fixing. No new main stock allocation or seam position has been silently changed after publication.

## 6. Anchor piles — complete rated assemblies, still not released

**Verified requirement:** §6.9.3 requires the specified bolted/threaded-rod detail or an alternative providing12 kN in the required directions along the bearer and joist. Plain ordinary pile nails are not a substitute.

MiTek's12KNH is a real304-stainless kit for low/exposed use. The manufacturer detail includes two160 mm pile/bearer plates, eightCT160 cleats shared across two joists, specified45 mm nails and skew nails. The kit's catalogue count is not a design instruction to put a cleat at an arbitrary joist or omit parts. Its drawings include nailing exclusions and85 mm minimum cleat installation space.

**Important drawing reading:** the square-pile figure says **25 degrees maximum scarf**, not25 mm notch depth. No25 mm-deep notch instruction is adopted. The actual pile/bearer face alignment and scarf geometry must be dimensioned from the selected manufacturer drawing. The current125-square piles and90 mm-wide doubled bearers cannot be assumed flush on both faces. Coordinate scarf processing, DPC and the lowest fastener/cut with the real finished ground before drilling/cutting.

Pryda PBK12S is also documented, but its190 mm plate and detailed scarf/nailing regions are a distinct assembly. Do not combine its nails or geometry with the MiTek kit. No new product substitution is selected by this review.

**Unresolved bounded check:** one complete anchor-pile/both-bearer-plies/adjacent-joists detail for the actual low frame, then apply it to the six candidate locations and verify the overall bracing design. The on-site guide alone does not establish all these project-specific dimensions. No main anchor positions are changed merely on a guess about the85 mm callout.

## 7. Tread-to-deck restraint — dimensioned, not yet rated

The owner-approved load path is tread→positive timber ties→main deck framing→its checked bracing/foundations. The tread's own posts carry its gravity load.

Existing dimensional proposal: fourH5 timber190 ×45 drop-cleats, about390 mm long; twoM10 through-bolts at each interface. There is a nominal50 mm horizontal gap to the rear tread bearer, and110 mm vertical gap from main rim bottom to tread framing top. The cleat/packer envelope can bridge these gaps.

**What is not established:** lateral design demand, fastener/group capacity in wet timber, cleat bending/tension, cross-grain splitting, washers and bolt lengths, rear-bearer ply force transfer, main-rim fixings/blocking and combined bracing. Ordinary two-nail rim-to-joist connections must not be assumed to transfer the entire tread bracing load. A bracket's steel rating or the bolt's steel shear strength does not establish the timber joint.

A qualified NZ structural designer needs to finish this connection. The current M10 sizes/count and cleat count remain review candidates, not instructions to buy or drill. No commercial rated detail verifying this complete offset assembly was found in the sources checked. No third party has been contacted.

## Outcome

The standard ordinary framing schedules, main ply-fastening geometry, required splice class and block-row proximity check are now documented from the full standard. The **two remaining bespoke connection tasks** are the complete low anchor/built-up-bearer detail and the tread ties/main-frame load transfer. Splice product application to both plies and load-carrying rebuilt short members must be covered in the final connection drawing rather than forgotten.

Actual soil, levels/drainage and the unknown pine product remain separate site/product evidence needs. Accepting50 mm overhang is an owner preference, not a board strength certificate. No question about the already-unknown product is repeated.
