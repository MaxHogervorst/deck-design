## Foundations

The eight holes are dug 400×400×600. Fill to 100 mm below the ground. The pile sits on 100 mm of concrete at the bottom, so this pour is about 0.59 m³. Twelve 20 kg bags of cement and 1 m³ of builders mix. Not premixed concrete bags. A truck minimum is still often 2.0 m³, which is more than the holes. Ask the yard the smallest load and the fee to take the rest back. The steps are a later pour.

The path stays. The deck is built over it. A 190 bearer with its top at +328 has its underside at +138, which is 12 mm under the 150 mm NZS 3604 wants for H3.2 above a surface. The grass is lower, so the piles there still clear 150 mm. No hole through the concrete. Do not open a bay past 2.7 m. The longest bay is 2.1 m.

The waste and the stormwater run together on the right of the path, the west side. The blue pipe is 400 mm past the concrete. The sewage is 130 mm past the blue one: the plan has one pipe 740 mm off the deck and the other 870 mm off the deck. Beside the deck they are about 440 to 470 mm deep, and only 240 to 270 mm deep beside the pad. The hole is 600 deep, so it goes below them. No hole edge within 500 mm of either pipe, and no hole between them. Stormwater goes out through the gate. The left of the path, the meter-box side, is clear. Orange is power, white is fibre, water is its own pipe. Dig with a spade.

### The two steps

A single 250 mm rise is too big. About 190 is the limit and 200 is a push. Two steps make three rises of about 167, and 2×167+280 comes out at 613, inside 550 to 700. No handrail at three rises.

Build the deck first. Then the bottom step, then the top step on it, set back 280 mm. Tape the rises off the path until they match. Do not cut a stringer.

The bottom framing only has about 135 mm above the path. The beam stays full depth, because Table 6.4 has no ripped size. At a loaded dimension under 1.30 m the same table allows a doubled 140 to span 3.0 m, continuous, and the longest step bay is 2.1 m. Where the timber meets the concrete, chase the path under it. Do not cantilever the joists off the bearer: they sit below it and they are only 500 mm long. The small pile sees about 30 kPa on a 300×300.

The underside of that bottom step is not 150 mm clear of the path. There is no way to have that and still walk off onto the path in even rises. It is H4 because it will stay damp. The deck bearer is H3.2 and sits 138 mm above the path, 12 mm short of the same 150 mm.

## Build order

1. Before you buy. Match the old deck: the new boards sit at that same 500 mm, not 500 mm above the low grass. Measure from the old boards down to the concrete path. Joists need about 330 mm of air over the path and the bearers about 190 mm. The steps are measured off the path as well, because that is where you walk. Tape that height and divide by three. If it is 500, each rise is 167. The lawn is lower and is not the step. Check the sill and the cladding. Formby Avenue is about 1.1 km from the harbour, outside the 500 m sea-spray zone, so the nails and the screws are galvanised. The two bolts stay stainless. A services reply that says no power within 10 m is not to be trusted: there is power. Mark the stormwater and the waste before any digging.
2. Set out. String the 6600×2600 outline. The rebuilt strip is 900 wide and runs 1970 back to the house wall. It has no holes. The steps stick out 560 mm past the front, two goings of 280. Leave the path. End joist flush, then 437 mm centres, doubled joist centred at 3956. Four pile lines, measured from the eastern end: 300, 2400, 4500, 6300, on both bearers. That is 300 mm past each end pile, then bays of 2100, 2100 and 1800. Eight holes. Paint the marks and shift a line if it lands on the path or within 500 mm of a pipe. The four step holes go on those same lines, 560 mm out, when the steps are built. Do not mark holes in the gap by the house.
3. Dig, with a spade. Every deck hole is 400×400 and 600 deep, down to firm ground, not topsoil. No 900 mm anchor holes. At 600 a shovel is enough. An auger only earns its keep past 600, and these holes do not go there. About ten minutes a hole. No hole in the concrete, no hole between the blue pipe and the sewage, and no hole edge within 500 mm of either. No spare hole on the pipe side of the path.
4. Concrete. The eight deck holes are this pour. Fill to 100 mm below the ground. The pile sits on 100 mm of concrete at the bottom, so the concrete is about 0.59 m³. Twelve 20 kg bags of cement and 1 m³ of builders mix, four shovels of mix to one of cement. The four step holes are later. Order 2.0 m³ only if the yard will not sell less, and ask what they charge to take the rest back. A hired mixer. Do not use premixed concrete bags. Nothing in the hole has to be level.
5. Cut the piles. One 6.0 m length makes the eight piles. String off the path, not the lawn. The bearer is 190 deep, so the pile tops finish at about +138, which is 190 mm below the bearer top at +328. That is 12 mm under the 150 mm NZS 3604 wants between H3.2 and the path. The grass is lower, so cut those piles first. Cut square, preservative on the cut, DPC on the top. Do not notch the bearer.
6. Bearers. Doubled 190×70 on both rows of the deck, the whole length (F1). Each ply is a 4.8 m length from the eastern end, butted on the pile at 4500, so that piece runs over the two 2.1 m bays. The remaining piece is the 1.8 m bay. Seat them and skew-nail (F2). No anchor brackets. Leave the existing bearer at the back edge. The rebuilt strip bolts to it at one end and to the house framing at the other.
7. Joists and rims. 17 joists of 2510: flush at each end, 437 centres, the doubled joist at 3956. Screws into a bearer go in from the side of the joist, not down through the top. The rebuilt strip is three joists of 2270, 45 mm beside the deck joists, from the house ledger to the first new bearer so the lap can be nailed (F4, F6). No blocks and no holes. Rims at Y0 and Y2555. Get the deck frame up before the steps. The step height is in mid-air until the deck is there. Offcuts can be a temporary step.
8. Step piles. Later, with the deck frame up. Dig the four small holes, 300×300 and 400 deep, on the same X as the front piles and 560 mm out from the face. Not 600. No extra hole beside the path. Stand a 750 mm pile, plumb it, and concrete it. Cut it so the front beam will finish with its top about 135 mm above the path.
9. Bottom step. Screw the inner ply of the H4 ribbon to the front piles, four 14g×100 screws each, then nail the outer ply to the inner one (F5). Joists out from it, cut about 500, on the same lines as the deck joists. Double front beam on the small piles, full depth, one piece so it runs over the piles. Top of this framing about 135 mm above the path, so the 32 mm board finishes at about 167. Do not rip it. Where the full depth meets the concrete, chase the path under the timber. A ripped beam is not a size in Table 6.4.
10. Top step, on the bottom one. Set it back one going, 280 mm. Double ribbon screwed to the face of the front bearer (F7), short joists cut about 240, front beam packed off the bottom step so the framing top is about 301 mm above the path. The board then finishes at about 333. Tape the three rises off the path and make them equal. Do not cut a stringer. No handrail. Three rises is the limit. One more would need a rail.
11. Boards. 28 rows on the deck, 21 on the rebuilt strip, three on each step, all along X. Butts on the doubled joist at 3956, so use the 4.2 m and 3.0 m lengths. A decking board on the face of the boundary joist, the top board over it, and a board on each riser. About 5 mm gaps on the deck and about 6 mm on the rebuilt strip. Two screws per board per joist. Preservative on every cut.

## Assumptions, and what I would get checked in an ideal world

We walked it on 11 November. The holes, the concrete and the two steps follow that conversation. It is still not an engineering sign-off, and there is no consent. Under 1.5 m the work is exempt. Exempt is not the same thing as the stair rules or the bracing rules being met on paper.

- **No anchor piles.** A freestanding deck needs something against racking. Four 900 mm anchors with brackets was the old default, and the ROBO preview counts bracing that way. On site, four anchors was more than this wants, and 600 mm on every pile was the call. I am following that. It is not a bracing calculation, and I am not buying the brackets.
- **The ground.** Firm clay at about 100 kPa under the topsoil. That is the usual Auckland assumption, not a test. A step pile is at about 30 kPa on a 300×300. If a hole is soft or wet at 600, that hole gets deeper or wider and it is no longer a spade job.
- **The steps.** The front beam is a doubled 140, full depth, continuous, and the longest bay is 2.1 m. Table 6.4(b) allows 3.0 m at a loaded dimension of 1.30 m. It is not ripped. Where it meets the path, the concrete is chased. The screws go through the inner ply only. The bottom step does not have 150 mm clearance over the path. The rises are even. They are not a consented stair. The four step holes are dug after the deck.
- **The services reply.** It said no gas and no power within 10 m. The power is there. Drain depths are approximate. Paint, spade, and move a hole rather than dig through a pipe.
- **Fixings.** Formby Avenue is about 1.1 km from the harbour, past the 500 m sea-spray line, so this is zone C. The nails and the screws are galvanised. The deck is open, so the two bolts stay 316. The decking is CCA. Do not swap in ACQ timber and keep these screws.
- **The path.** It stays. The piles are on a regular grid, 300 mm in from each end, then 2.1 m, 2.1 m and 1.8 m. Paint them and move a hole if it lands on the concrete or within 500 mm of a pipe. Both bearers are doubled 190×70. Table 6.4(b) allows one 190×70 to span 2.7 m, and the longest bay is 2.1 m. The steps are taped off the path, not off the lawn.
- **Prices.** Auckland only, read on 12 November 2026. Marton, Horotiu and Stratford are left out. Each line is the cheapest posted price, and the store is named. The H4 line is 150×50 SG8 because 140×45 H4 is not listed. N2 H4 is cheaper and is the wrong grade. The truck is the figure from site, not a Point Chevalier quote.
- **The rebuilt strip.** No holes. The joists run from the house ledger to the first new bearer, 45 mm beside the deck joists, and the lap is nailed. The long span is 1970 mm and Table 7.1(b) allows 2350 mm. Bolt the ledger into the framing and flash it. Do not fix it to the weatherboards. The main deck is on its own eight piles.
